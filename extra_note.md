# Extra note on selected figures and table entries

The report intentionally does not display every available CSV row or every possible channel-level result. Instead, it selects representative figures and table entries that best support interpretation of the reproduction attempt.

## Why the rhythm figures focus on channel-level contrasts

The rhythm-contrast figures show `regular mean ITPC - irregular mean ITPC` for each EEG channel at 2 Hz and 4 Hz. These plots were selected because the main research question concerns whether regular poetic rhythm produces stronger entrainment than irregular poetic rhythm. Showing the contrast directly makes the direction of the effect visible: values below zero indicate stronger entrainment for irregular lines.

These figures include all channels, so they are useful for assessing whether the result is localized to the paper's highlighted sites or appears broadly across the scalp.

## Why Pz, C4, and T7 are emphasized

Pz, C4, and T7 are shown separately because the paper draft identifies these channels as central evidence for the 2 Hz regular-rhythm effect. They are therefore the most important candidate channels for direct comparison between the paper and the reproduction.

The focused Pz/C4/T7 table is not meant to replace the full channel table. It is included because these channels test the paper's main claim most directly.

## Why only the strongest rhythm effects are listed

The rhythm-effect table in the report lists selected strongest effects rather than all rows from the full result table. This is because the complete table is available in the linked CSV, while the report needs to remain readable. The selected entries show the scale and frequency distribution of the reproduced rhythm effects without overwhelming the reader with every channel.

The selection is descriptive: it highlights where the reproduced rhythm effects are largest, not necessarily where the paper placed greatest theoretical emphasis.

## Why the group-effect comparison uses selected channels

The group-effect comparison focuses on channels and frequencies that the paper draft reports as evidence for non-English/Romanian monolingual participants showing greater entrainment. These are the relevant candidates for testing whether the reproduced group pattern matches the paper.

The table compares the paper-reported difference with the reproduced difference for those candidates. This makes it clear whether the reproduction gives evidence in the same direction, weaker evidence, or the opposite direction.

## Why not all non-significant results are shown

Many reproduced group effects are non-significant and close to zero. Listing all of them in the main report would add length without improving interpretation. Instead, the report shows the smallest reproduced p-values and the paper-relevant candidate channels. The full results remain available in the linked CSV files for inspection.

In short, the report prioritizes candidates that are either theoretically central in the paper or most informative for judging whether the reproduction supports the same conclusions.
